Texas value golf can mean a $25 walking round in Lubbock, a weekday municipal in Dallas or Austin, or a more expensive East Texas course whose included cart and practice balls make the whole day worthwhile. This shortlist mixes affordable repeat-play golf with one premium-value upgrade. It is not a list of the ten lowest prices in the state.

This is Break 90 Golf’s research-based editorial shortlist, not an official statewide ranking or firsthand condition review. We select for course character, visitor access and a useful complete-round price proposition. Separate layouts at one property may appear individually.

**Rates and booking information checked October 15, 2026.** Prices are USD per player unless stated otherwise. They are published references, not guaranteed quotes. Tax, cart, caddie, gratuity, priority-booking fees and lodging are extra unless explicitly included. A live or resort-package quote is required where no reliable flat current rate is published. Check maintenance, restrictions and cancellation terms before traveling.

Compare our [Texas no-budget-limit shortlist](/articles/top-public-golf-courses-texas/), or explore all [state course guides](/courses/).

**Budget with the visitor price.** The figures below use ordinary adult public prices, not senior, junior, annual-pass or local-resident starting rates. Memorial Park can be excellent for eligible Houston residents, but we do not use its discounted resident fee to promise visitors the same value.

## 1. Brackenridge Park Golf Course

**San Antonio**

Brackenridge is the heritage pick in San Antonio’s municipal system. Its historic setting and full-length course make it worth comparing with resort golf nearby, especially if you want a round that leaves time for the city. Use the public column rather than a pass-holder discount to budget a visit.

**Price:** **Public regular 18-hole walk/ride rates: $56/$75 Monday–Thursday; $68/$87 Friday–Sunday and holidays.** Public twilight: $40/$51 or $46/$57. Confirm tax and the seasonal start time.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Make a plan for position rather than maximum tee-shot distance. Accept a bogey if a wayward shot leaves you recovering.

[Official course information](https://alamocitygolftrail.com/brackenridge-park-golf-course/) · [Official rates / booking](https://alamocitygolftrail.com/green-fees/)

## 2. Gus Wortham Golf Course

**Houston**

Gus Wortham is a strong Houston comparison when you want public golf without relying on a city-resident discount. Its Houston Golf Association restoration and public access make it a practical candidate for repeat visits as well as a golf day during a city trip.

**Price:** **Walking green fee: $38 Monday–Thursday / $47 Friday–Sunday and holidays; shared cart adds $17 per person.** Tax extra. Optional 8–10-day priority booking adds a nonrefundable $20 per player.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose a comfortable tee, then track the same mistakes over a few rounds. Familiarity helps you judge whether a different decision works.

[Official course information](https://www.hga.org/gus-wortham-golf-course) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.hga.org/gus-wortham/rates)

## 3. Cedar Crest Golf Course

**Dallas**

A.W. Tillinghast’s Cedar Crest gives Dallas golfers a municipal course with design history and a clear walking discount. It makes sense for a day focused on the course rather than a resort package, with the published regular rates providing an accessible starting comparison.

**Price:** **Regular walk/ride rates: $31/$52 Monday–Thursday; $35/$56 Friday–Sunday and holidays.** Twilight and sundown discounts vary by season. Confirm tax at checkout.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Play to the part of the fairway that leaves a clear approach. Use the walking option if it suits your energy and the weather.

[Official course information](https://www.golfcedarcrest.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.golfcedarcrest.com/golf-course/course-rates)

## 4. Stevens Park Golf Course

**Dallas**

Mature oaks and changing elevation give Stevens Park a different city-golf character. It is another useful Dallas choice when the course matters more than resort extras. Its operator separates walking from shared-cart prices and provides time-of-day savings, so the value calculation can fit the time you actually have available.

**Price:** **Regular morning walk/ride: $35/$56 Monday–Thursday; $48/$69 Friday–Sunday and holidays.** Operator noon twilight rates are $28/$49 and $35/$56. Confirm tax and booking rules.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Favor a playable landing spot over forcing a long tee shot. Leave yourself enough energy for careful approach and putting decisions.

[Official course information](https://www.stevensparkgolf.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.stevensparkgolf.com/course/rates)

## 5. Lions Municipal Golf Course

**Austin**

Known locally as Muny, Lions brings Austin golf history into an ordinary municipal budget. It is a useful central-city choice for a visitor who wants a full round without turning the day into a resort excursion. Check the current tee sheet rather than assuming a historic course will be easy to book.

**Price:** **Regular green fee: $35 Monday–Thursday, $41 Friday, $44 weekends/holidays; cart $18 per person extra.** Confirm tax and the evening/sunset calendar. Resident-card discounts are separate.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose a club that leaves a clear next shot. A short course on the scorecard does not make every aggressive line worthwhile.

[Official course information](https://www.austintexas.gov/golfatx/lions-municipal-course) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.austintexas.gov/golfatx/fees-memberships)

## 6. Roy Kizer Golf Course

**Austin**

Roy Kizer offers an open, links-style contrast within Austin’s municipal group. The city’s published ordinary green fees make the comparison straightforward, while the setting gives you a different round from Lions rather than simply another version of the same course.

**Price:** **Regular green fee: $35 Monday–Thursday, $41 Friday, $44 weekends/holidays; cart $18 per person extra.** Confirm tax and the evening/sunset calendar. Resident-card discounts are separate.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Check the wind on the open holes. Choose the safe side of water and use a dependable carry distance.

[Official course information](https://www.austintexas.gov/golfatx/roy-kizer-course) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.austintexas.gov/golfatx/fees-memberships)

## 7. Morris Williams Golf Course

**Austin**

Leon Howard’s Morris Williams course uses rolling terrain and elevated, contoured greens east of downtown Austin. It is another full-length public option at the same regular green-fee level. It earns a spot because a practical city-golf rotation should offer more than one location and tee sheet, allowing you to match the round to your schedule instead of paying for a resort you do not need.

**Price:** **Regular green fee: $35 Monday–Thursday, $41 Friday, $44 weekends/holidays; cart $18 per person extra.** Confirm tax and the evening/sunset calendar. Resident-card discounts are separate.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose the course and time that leave room to warm up. Rushing to a bargain tee time can undo the benefit of the lower bill.

[Official course information](https://www.austintexas.gov/golfatx/morris-williams-course) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.austintexas.gov/golfatx/fees-memberships)

## 8. Butterfield Trail Golf Club

**El Paso**

Butterfield Trail makes El Paso part of the value conversation with a Tom Fazio design and clearly posted public price windows. For a traveler already in far West Texas, it is a much more sensible comparison than driving across the state to save a few dollars somewhere else.

**Price:** **Posted 18-hole public morning rates: $39.95 weekdays / $49.95 weekends, plus tax.** The operator’s course-information page says carts are included in the green fee. After-noon prices are lower; confirm the selected time.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Play for the conditions and the lie. Keep the next target simple when the wind or sand takes away your preferred shot.

[Official course information](https://www.butterfieldtrailgolf.com/golf/course-information) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.butterfieldtrailgolf.com/golf/course-rates)

## 9. Meadowbrook — Canyon Course

**Lubbock**

Meadowbrook’s Canyon Course adds a clearly priced municipal option in Lubbock. The original Meadowbrook layout uses rolling, tree-lined fairways and changing greens, giving a South Plains trip another choice alongside the higher-profile Rawls Course.

**Price:** **2026 before-2 p.m. walk/ride rates: $25/$41 Monday–Thursday; $33/$49 Friday–Sunday and holidays, including tax.** Call for walking reservations; weekend bookings require prepayment.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Treat the opening long par 5 as a series of manageable shots. There is no need to win the round on the first hole.

[Official course information](https://www.meadowbrooklubbock.com/Course/Canyon) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.meadowbrooklubbock.com/Rates)

## 10. Pine Dunes Resort & Golf Club

**Frankston**

Pine Dunes is the premium-value upgrade on this list rather than the cheapest round. Its $99 weekday rate includes cart and range balls, making it a useful East Texas comparison when you want a destination-style day without a mandatory resort-hotel bill. It also appears in our no-budget-limit guide.

**Price:** **$99 Monday–Thursday / $149 Friday–Sunday**, including green fee, cart and range balls. After-4 p.m. twilight is $79/$99. Confirm tax and available daylight.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Compare the total included weekday price with local alternatives, then choose tees for comfortable approaches. A later twilight price only works if you have enough daylight.

[Official course information](https://www.pinedunes.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.pinedunes.com/golf/rates-fees)

## Plan Texas golf by region

Dallas–Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, Houston, East Texas, Lubbock and the Big Bend area are different travel bases. Start with the city or region you will actually visit. Black Jack’s Crossing and El Paso belong to far-west itineraries, not a casual add-on to an Austin weekend.

For a Dallas comparison, look at Cedar Crest and Stevens Park. Around Austin, compare Lions, Roy Kizer and Morris Williams on the same day. In Lubbock, Meadowbrook gives you a low-cost walking option. The list is deliberately strongest around practical city golf; it is not an exhaustive directory of inexpensive courses in every Texas region.

Check the forecast, wind, heat, daylight and any course-maintenance notice. Later tee times can be cheaper, but a discount does not guarantee that you can finish 18 holes. Ask about the final checkout total, walking/cart rules and what happens if weather interrupts play.

On the course, use the [math of breaking 90](/articles/break-90-golf-scoring-math-find-lost-strokes/) and choose tees for your normal carry distance. Keep the next shot playable and let the destination be memorable without making every shot heroic.
