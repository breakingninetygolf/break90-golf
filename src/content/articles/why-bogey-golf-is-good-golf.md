Bogey is a useful score when your target is to break 90. That does not mean aiming to play badly. It means choosing a plan that keeps one ordinary mistake from becoming a double or triple.

## What bogey golf means on par 72

Eighteen bogeys on a par-72 course total 90. Replace one of those bogeys with a par and the total is 89. This example changes on a course with a different par, so check the scorecard before using it as your round target.

You do not have to manufacture exactly that card. The example simply shows why a round in the 80s can include many imperfect shots. It also gives you permission to accept a bogey without trying to win the stroke back immediately.

## Protect the next shot

Suppose your tee shot finishes behind a tree. You can see a narrow gap toward the green, but the ball is sitting poorly and branches limit your swing. The useful question is not whether you have ever pulled off that shot. It is which option leaves you a repeatable next shot today.

A recovery into open space may leave a chance to reach the green with the following stroke. A failed attempt through the gap can leave the same problem again. Judge the decision by the information available before you swing, rather than by whether one attempt happens to work.

## Make a bogey plan before trouble arrives

For a hole with a difficult carry, consider whether your chosen tees fit your normal distance. If they do, plan a comfortable target. If they do not, choose a more suitable tee set before the round rather than spending eighteen holes attempting maximum-distance swings.

For a long par 4, a safe approach short of the green may leave a straightforward chip when reaching in two is unrealistic. Know where that short shot can finish safely. A bunker or penalty area can make the obvious lay-up a poor choice.

## Avoid turning the score into pressure

A par does not give you permission to take an unnecessary risk on the next tee. A double does not mean the round is over. Return to the same sequence: assess the lie, choose the target, choose the club and commit.

Try this approach for nine holes. Track penalties and three-putts alongside the total, then review your decisions after the round. Review your last few scorecards for recurring mistakes, and keep the [math of breaking 90](/#break-90-math) in mind.

## Take one task to practice

If your notes show repeated long first putts followed by difficult second putts, work on [distance control](/articles/stop-three-putting/). If tee shots bring trouble into play, review [when to use driver](/articles/should-you-hit-driver-on-every-par-four/). Choose one focus rather than changing everything because of a single scorecard.
