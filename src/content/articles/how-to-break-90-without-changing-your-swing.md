Breaking 90 does not require every part of your game to improve at once. Start by making the round you already play easier to manage: choose comfortable tees, keep penalty areas out of your normal miss and give yourself a straightforward next shot.

## Start with the math of 89

On a par-72 course, seventeen bogeys and one par total 89. Eighteen bogeys total 90. That is one useful way to picture the target, not a rule that you must follow on every hole. Birdies, pars and doubles will still appear on real scorecards.

The point is to stop treating a routine bogey as a reason to attempt a risky shot on the next hole. Review your last few scorecards to see how penalties, three-putts and avoidable short-game shots fit into your own round.

## Choose targets for your usual shot

Before a tee shot, identify where your normal shot tends to finish. Choose an aiming point with room around that pattern, rather than selecting a narrow line that works only when you hit your best drive.

On approaches, start with the middle of the green. Adjust only when there is a clear reason, such as a hazard beside that target or a distance you cannot comfortably carry. A flag is information; it does not have to be your aiming point.

## Driver is an option, not an obligation

Compare the landing areas for driver and a shorter club. A hybrid is not automatically safer if it leaves the same hazard in play or a much harder approach. Pick the club that gives your normal shot a useful place to finish. Our [tee-club decision guide](/articles/should-you-hit-driver-on-every-par-four/) walks through that choice.

## Stop a mistake from becoming a sequence

After a poor drive, ask what shot you can repeat from the lie you have. A clear route back to the fairway may be preferable to a narrow opening between trees. Plan the recovery around the next approach, not the shot you wish you had hit.

Near the green, use a familiar shot toward a generous landing area. When putting is practical, compare it with a chip rather than automatically choosing the more complicated option.

## Your next-round assignment

Record three things after each hole: penalties, three-putts and avoidable recovery mistakes. Do not rebuild your swing during the round. Review those notes afterward, choose one task and give it a week of focused practice.

Start with the [30-minute practice plan](/articles/30-minute-practice-plan/) or the [lag-putting drill](/articles/stop-three-putting/). The goal is a repeatable routine that gives you feedback, not a promise that the next score will be 89.
