The best Wisconsin golf for the money is not one price bracket. The Bog leads our premium-value choices, while Milwaukee-area municipal courses bring the total down considerably. This list mixes affordable repeat-play rounds with a few design-focused upgrades whose value depends on the tee time you choose.

These are Break 90 Golf’s research-based editorial selections, not an official ranking or firsthand condition review. We considered course character, booking access and the complete cost of the outing. Separate layouts at one property appear individually.

**Rates and access checked October 15, 2026.** Prices are USD per player unless stated otherwise. Posted rates are references, not guaranteed quotes. Taxes, carts, caddies, gratuities, park entry and lodging are extra unless explicitly included. Where the operator does not publish a dependable current flat rate, we say **quote required** rather than invent a price. Confirm maintenance, seasonal closure and cancellation terms before traveling.

Compare our [Wisconsin no-budget-limit guide](/articles/top-public-golf-courses-wisconsin/), or browse all [state course guides](/courses/).

**Season planning:** Wisconsin’s season is weather-dependent. Late tee times may not leave enough daylight for 18 holes, and a published rate does not mean the course is open today. Check frost delays, aeration and closing dates directly.

## 1. The Bog

**Saukville**

Arnold Palmer’s public layout runs through woods, wetlands and rolling ground north of Milwaukee. This is our premium-value pick rather than the state’s lowest-price round: compare the off-peak windows with a headline-resort bill. It gives a group a substantial destination-style option without needing a resort stay.

**Price:** **Posted late-season rates, October 12–close: $75 Monday–Wednesday; $85 Thursday–Sunday before 2 p.m., then $75.** Prime-season weekday twilight after 3 p.m. is $60 walking, with an optional $15 cart-seat fee. Add 5.5% sales tax; confirm late-season cart inclusion.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose a tee that makes the carries comfortable. Protect your score around wetlands and give yourself a playable next shot.

[Official course information](https://www.golfthebog.com/course_info/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.golfthebog.com/2023-seasonal-rates/)

## 2. Washington County Golf Course

**Hartford**

Washington County makes its value case with a clear nonresident rate schedule and time-of-day savings. It is a practical comparison near the Erin/Hartford golf area when your group wants another full round without repeating a championship green fee.

**Price:** **May 18–November 1, 2026 nonresident 18-hole rates: $53 walking / $77 riding weekdays; $68/$92 weekends.** Published weekday-before-10 a.m. special: $59 riding, excluding holidays. After-3 p.m. twilight: $35 walking / $59 riding.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Check daylight before choosing twilight. The lower price is useful only if the timing fits your round.

[Official course information](https://www.golfwcgc.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.golfwcgc.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/8194/2026/01/Rates-May-18-Nov-1st-2026.pdf)

## 3. Brown Deer Golf Course

**Milwaukee**

Brown Deer offers a tournament-pedigree municipal option inside the Milwaukee County system. It is not the cheapest county course, but it belongs in a value comparison for golfers who want a recognizable, demanding full-length round without luxury-resort pricing.

**Price:** **Standard 18-hole green fee: $81; cart $20 extra.** Discount-card rates are separate and require eligibility. Confirm taxes and the final checkout total.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Treat the longer holes as three-shot routes to a bogey when needed. Do not let the course’s reputation choose your tee box.

[Official course information](https://mke.golf/browndeer/)

## 4. Naga-Waukee War Memorial Golf Course

**Pewaukee / Delafield area**

A county-operated option west of Milwaukee, Naga-Waukee gives visitors a published nonresident comparison instead of requiring a local card to reach the advertised starting price. It suits a trip that mixes one premium day with a more restrained second round.

**Price:** **Nonresident fall green fee, September 28–close: $51 for 18; cart extra.** Summer nonresident green fees are $52 weekdays / $55 weekends and holidays. Resident discounts are separate.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Compare the full riding total with nearby choices. A comfortable tee selection is more valuable than stretching the course for its own sake.

[Official course information](https://www.waukeshacounty.gov/parks-and-land-use/parks-and-recreation/golf/naga-waukee-golf-course/)

## 5. Whitnall Golf Course

**Franklin**

Whitnall brings wooded fairways and changing elevation into a municipal budget. Its posted back-tee length is not extreme, yet the terrain and greens create enough interest to make a careful round rewarding. This is a good candidate for keeping more golf in the weekly budget.

**Price:** **Standard 18-hole green fee: $44; cart $18 extra.** Cardholder prices are lower but are not the visitor baseline.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Give tree-lined holes a realistic target. A clean fairway recovery is preferable to trying to save par through branches.

[Official course information](https://mke.golf/whitnall/)

## 6. Dretzka Golf Course

**Milwaukee**

Dretzka combines tree-lined holes, elevation changes and water crossings with the same standard green-fee level as Whitnall. Four sets of tees give a group room to select a suitable test. Its value is the amount of course interest available at an ordinary municipal price.

**Price:** **Standard 18-hole green fee: $44; cart $18 extra.** Weekend reservations before 11 a.m. are for 18 holes.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Check the forced carry on the opening hole from your chosen tee. Plan where a safe miss leaves you.

[Official course information](https://mke.golf/dretzka/)

## 7. Currie Golf Course

**Wauwatosa**

Currie is a sensible lower-cost choice for golfers who prefer wider landing areas to an intimidating resort test. The county describes inviting fairways and undulating greens, giving you space to work on decisions while still requiring attention near the hole.

**Price:** **Standard 18-hole green fee: $38; cart $18 extra.** Check the current road-access notice and weekend reservation restrictions.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Use the room from the tee to choose a comfortable club. Make distance control on the greens your main scoring task.

[Official course information](https://mke.golf/currie/)

## 8. Lawsonia — Woodlands Course

**Green Lake**

Woodlands deserves its own look rather than being treated as the spare course at Lawsonia. It gives a multi-round trip a contrasting layout at the same property. Its value depends on the actual quote, particularly when compared with the better-known Links.

**Price:** **Live quote required.** Compare Woodlands with Links on the same date and ask for the walking/cart breakdown. No fixed low-price promise is made here.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Keep your tee shot in a position that leaves a clear approach instead of choosing club solely for distance.

[Official course information](https://www.lawsonia.com/the-woodlands) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.lawsonia.com/book-golf)

## 9. University Ridge Golf Course

**Madison**

The University of Wisconsin’s public course is a premium-value option at the lower end of its dynamic range. It is a useful Madison anchor for golfers who want a destination-quality outing without assuming the highest-demand slot is the only way to play.

**Price:** **Operator’s typical dynamic 18-hole riding range: $75–$145**, including warm-up range balls during the 45 minutes before play. Actual price varies by date, time and demand.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Compare several tee times before choosing a date. Use the included warm-up to establish contact and pace, not to rebuild your swing.

[Official course information](https://www.universityridge.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.universityridge.com/rates)

## 10. Lawsonia — Links Course

**Green Lake**

The Links appears in both Wisconsin guides because value can mean an especially interesting design for the total cost, not just the smallest number on a rate sheet. Put it beside a municipal round when building a varied trip rather than assuming every day needs a resort booking.

**Price:** **Live quote required.** Include walking, cart and replay arrangements in the comparison; it is an architecture-value candidate, not a guaranteed budget round.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead.

**Break 90 approach:** Leave the ball in a position that gives you a simple next shot, especially around demanding green surrounds.

## Plan a round that fits your trip

Keep the travel sensible: pair Kohler/Sheboygan rounds together, use Green Lake for Lawsonia, and leave a separate block for Nekoosa or Stevens Point. The value list is deliberately strongest around Milwaukee and southeastern Wisconsin; it is not an evenly distributed statewide budget directory. Compare walking totals with riding totals, and do not use a resident-card price as your visitor budget.

Before booking, ask about green aeration, the tees and holes currently available, cart restrictions, the full checkout total and cancellation terms. Confirm that the booking is for the course named here when a property has multiple layouts.

For your score, use the [math of breaking 90](/articles/break-90-golf-scoring-math-find-lost-strokes/) and choose tees that fit your normal carry distance. A memorable round does not require playing the back tees.
