## LPGA Shanghai: Choi stays ahead on a difficult Friday

The Buick LPGA Shanghai runs October 15–18 at Sheshan Golf Club. Choi followed an opening 64 with a 74 and still held a two-shot advantage at six under after two rounds. Her position captures the difference between Thursday's scoring opportunities and a tougher Friday.

### Leading scores after round two

| Position | Player | Total relative to par | Second round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hye-Jin Choi | −6 | 74 |
| T2 | Amy Yang | −4 | 71 |
| T2 | Grace Kim | −4 | 72 |

Yang and Kim handled the windy conditions well enough to keep Choi within reach. Kim's level-par round contained two birdies and two bogeys; Yang gained a stroke against par. Neither needed a spectacular score to remain a serious contender.

**What to watch Saturday:** Does Choi recover her opening-round scoring, or does another difficult day bring the chasers level? From a viewer's perspective, watch how the leaders respond after a dropped shot. That decision is often more useful to an everyday golfer than the next highlight-reel approach.

## India: the clubhouse lead is not a completed halfway lead

Storms cost nearly five hours on Thursday, leaving players to make up ground on Friday at Delhi Golf Club. Darkness interrupted play again, so a finished second-round score and an unfinished one must be treated differently.

### Selected contenders at Friday's suspension

| Player | Tournament score | Round-two status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Angel Hidalgo | −8 | Finished: 68–68 |
| Joost Luiten | −8 | Finished: 68–68 |
| Adrian Meronk | −8 | Finished: second-round 69 |
| Thomas Detry | −8 | Through six holes of round two |
| Eddie Pepperell | −7 | Finished: 71–66 |
| Eugenio Chacarra | −7 | Through five holes of round two |

This is a selected snapshot, not a complete leaderboard. Hidalgo, Luiten and Meronk shared the clubhouse lead. Detry still had 12 holes of his second round available to change the picture. Chacarra also had plenty left to play.

Pepperell's 66 put him one behind the clubhouse leaders. Daniel Hillier produced another notable recovery: after a 74, he came back with a 64 to reach six under. Rory McIlroy made an eagle at the start of his second round, but his round was also interrupted. Patrick Reed finished at level par for the tournament and had to wait on the cut outcome.

**What to watch Saturday:** The first meaningful development is the completion of round two. Check the final cut and the status of every contender before comparing the third-round groups. A player with holes in hand can change the apparent lead without anyone in the clubhouse hitting another shot.

## SAS Championship: Goosen sets the opening pace

The SAS Championship is the PGA TOUR Champions event at Prestonwood Country Club in Cary, North Carolina, October 16–18. Unlike the two events above, Friday was the first competitive round; Saturday is round two, with the final round on Sunday.

### Leading scores after round one

| Position | Player | Opening score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Retief Goosen | 65 (−7) |
| 2 | Richard Green | 66 (−6) |
| 3 | Tag Ridings | 67 (−5) |

Goosen's seven-under start gives him a one-shot edge as he pursues another victory this season. He entered the week fifth in the Charles Schwab Cup standings, according to the tour's Friday report. Green is immediately behind him, with Ridings another stroke back.

**What to watch Saturday:** Whether Goosen can build separation or Green and Ridings bring him back toward the field. With only one round completed, the opening order is an early advantage rather than a settled weekend hierarchy.

## Where is the regular PGA TOUR this weekend?

The regular PGA TOUR has an off week. Its published fall calendar marks October 12–18 as off, following the October 8–11 Baycurrent Classic in Japan. A general PGA TOUR leaderboard page may therefore still show last week's event. Do not mistake that result for a tournament being played this Saturday.

## A Break 90 way to watch the weekend

Pick one player and watch the less glamorous decisions: the recovery after trouble, the safe side of an approach, and the response to an ordinary bogey. Our takeaway is an observation task, not a claim that a particular professional used a particular strategy this week.

Before your next round, choose one habit to copy. Keep the ball in play after a mistake. Give a long putt enough attention to avoid a careless three-putt. Let a difficult hole end with a manageable score instead of trying to win the stroke back immediately.
