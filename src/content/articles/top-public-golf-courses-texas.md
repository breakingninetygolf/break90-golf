Texas has more than one kind of destination golf: remote Big Bend scenery, championship golf in Frisco, East Texas pines, South Plains wind and Hill Country resort courses. These are our ten public-access picks when the experience matters more than the lowest green fee. Some are daily-fee public courses; others require an eligible resort stay.

This is Break 90 Golf’s research-based editorial shortlist, not an official statewide ranking or firsthand condition review. We select for course character, visitor access and a varied and memorable golf trip. Separate layouts at one property may appear individually.

**Rates and booking information checked October 15, 2026.** Prices are USD per player unless stated otherwise. They are published references, not guaranteed quotes. Tax, cart, caddie, gratuity, priority-booking fees and lodging are extra unless explicitly included. A live or resort-package quote is required where no reliable flat current rate is published. Check maintenance, restrictions and cancellation terms before traveling.

Compare our [Texas best-value course guide](/articles/best-value-public-golf-courses-texas/), or explore all [state course guides](/courses/).

**Public-access does not always mean walk-up golf.** [Barton Creek](https://www.omnihotels.com/hotels/austin-barton-creek/golf/rates) is exclusive to registered resort guests and members; [TPC San Antonio](https://tpc.com/sanantonio/about/) also requires eligible resort-guest or member access. Fields Ranch offers a separate public booking window. Confirm the course and tee time before paying for a hotel.

## 1. Black Jack's Crossing

**Lajitas**

Lanny Wadkins’s course near Big Bend is the remote-desert choice on this list. Mountain views and the Rio Grande setting give the round a sense of occasion that is difficult to reproduce near a major city. Build a real West Texas visit around it rather than treating it as a short detour.

**Price:** **Seasonal quote required** from the golf shop. Confirm the green fee, cart and practice inclusions, tax, and any guest or lodging requirements.

**Access:** Visitor/resort golf by reservation; confirm golf-only eligibility and seasonal availability directly.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose tees for your normal carry, not the view from the back tee. Leave room for wind and uneven ground.

[Official course information](https://www.lajitasgolfresort.com/golf/)

## 2. Fields Ranch — East Course

**Frisco**

Gil Hanse’s East Course is the championship-focused half of PGA Frisco. Rolling ground, Panther Creek and demanding green targets create a serious golf day. It belongs on the shortlist for a group that wants to experience a major-championship venue while choosing a sensible version of the test.

**Price:** **Live public quote required.** The checked schedule requires double-bag caddies and no carts September 18–November 29; confirm caddie fees, gratuity and tax.

**Access:** Public booking window listed as seven days; member and resort channels have separate terms. Confirm current walking/caddie rules.

**Break 90 approach:** Use the caddie’s help to choose a useful target. A long hole can still be played as a controlled three-shot route to bogey.

[Official course information](https://fieldsranch.com/golf)

## 3. Pine Dunes Resort & Golf Club

**Frankston**

Pine Dunes gives East Texas a destination-style round in a pine-forest setting without requiring a luxury hotel booking. It is a useful stop for a trip built around golf itself rather than the extras of a large resort. The published weekday rate also earns it a place in our value guide.

**Price:** **$99 Monday–Thursday / $149 Friday–Sunday**, including green fee, cart and range balls. After-4 p.m. twilight is $79/$99. Confirm tax and available daylight.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Pick tees that make the approach distances manageable. Recover into a clear opening when the trees take away the direct shot.

[Official course information](https://www.pinedunes.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.pinedunes.com/golf/rates-fees)

## 4. The Rawls Course at Texas Tech

**Lubbock**

The Rawls Course gives the South Plains part of the itinerary a substantial public-golf anchor. Its Texas Tech setting makes it a natural choice for a Lubbock visit, while its public tee-time channel means you do not need a student membership to arrange a round.

**Price:** **Dynamic quote required.** Ordinary listed green fees include cart and range balls; junior cart rules differ. Confirm tax and the public booking category.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Watch what the wind is doing to your comfortable carry distance. Favor repeatable contact over swinging harder into it.

[Official course information](https://www.therawlscourse.com/) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.therawlscourse.com/rates/)

## 5. Omni Barton Creek — Fazio Canyons

**Austin**

Fazio Canyons is one of the central reasons to choose Barton Creek for a Hill Country golf stay. It provides a separate layout from Foothills, so a group can compare two Tom Fazio courses rather than expecting a second day to be a repeat of the first.

**Price:** **Seasonal resort-guest quote required.** The golf fee includes shared cart, practice-facility use and range balls. Lodging, taxes and other resort charges require confirmation.

**Access:** Exclusive to registered Omni Barton Creek resort guests and country-club members; arrange golf before paying for accommodation.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose the safe side of the approach target and account for the lie. A difficult recovery is a good time to stop chasing par.

[Official course information](https://www.omnihotels.com/hotels/austin-barton-creek/golf/fazio-canyons) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.omnihotels.com/hotels/austin-barton-creek/golf/rates)

## 6. Omni Barton Creek — Fazio Foothills

**Austin**

Foothills offers another full-length Fazio experience at Barton Creek. It is a compelling choice when the appeal is several golf days from one Austin-area base, with resort amenities available to the group outside the round.

**Price:** **Seasonal resort-guest quote required.** Published inclusions are shared cart, practice access and range balls; confirm the complete golf-and-stay total.

**Access:** Registered resort guests and members only. A restaurant visit or ordinary day pass does not establish golf access.

**Break 90 approach:** Select tees that leave familiar approach clubs. Keep the next shot in mind when choosing where to miss.

[Official course information](https://www.omnihotels.com/hotels/austin-barton-creek/golf/fazio-foothills) · [Official rates / booking](https://www.omnihotels.com/hotels/austin-barton-creek/golf/rates)

## 7. TPC San Antonio — Oaks Course

**San Antonio**

The Oaks is the resort-access championship choice for a San Antonio golf stay. Pairing golf with the adjacent JW Marriott can make sense for a mixed golf-and-family trip, but the accommodation requirement belongs in the budget even when green fees are secondary.

**Price:** **Eligible hotel-guest or stay-and-play quote required.** Confirm Oaks availability, the golf charge, cart or caddie arrangements, tax and the full lodging bill.

**Access:** Private resort course accessible to eligible JW Marriott San Antonio guests and club members; not unrestricted public day golf.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose a tee that keeps the required carries comfortable. Give hazards a wider margin than you would on a familiar home course.

[Official course information](https://tpc.com/sanantonio/) · [Official rates / booking](https://tpc.com/sanantonio/vacations/)

## 8. Fields Ranch — West Course

**Frisco**

Beau Welling’s West Course gives PGA Frisco a different personality from the East. Its rolling prairie setting and shot-making focus make it a useful companion round, especially for a group that wants two distinct layouts while keeping the same travel base.

**Price:** **Live public quote required.** Compare West with East on your date and confirm cart inclusion, any caddie option and tax.

**Access:** Public tee times are listed with a seven-day booking window. Walking and carts are permitted under the current course rules; confirm daily restrictions.

**Break 90 approach:** Choose where you want the approach to start from. A shorter club can be useful when it leaves a better angle.

[Official course information](https://fieldsranch.com/golf)

## 9. Lost Pines Golf Club (formerly Wolfdancer)

**Lost Pines / Bastrop area**

The Arthur Hills design now promoted as Lost Pines Golf Club adds a woodland-and-river-area alternative east of Austin. Its combination of golf and resort facilities can suit a trip that leaves space for activities beyond the course. Older listings may still call it Wolfdancer.

**Price:** **Live tee-time quote required** from the official booking link. Confirm the day-golfer total, shared cart, practice access and tax rather than using an old Wolfdancer price.

**Access:** Public tee times; reserve ahead and confirm current availability.

**Break 90 approach:** Do not treat the resort setting as a reason to play farther back. Choose a tee and target that keep the ball in play.

[Official course information](https://www.lostpinesresortandspa.com/golf/) · [Current name and location](https://explorebastropcounty.com/listings/wolfdancer-golf-club/)

## 10. Golf Club of Houston — Tournament Course

**Humble**

The Rees Jones and David Toms design gives Houston visitors a public route onto a course with professional-tournament history. The club also has a Member Course, so the booking distinction matters as much as the name on the entrance sign.

**Price:** **Live public tee-time quote required.** Specify the Tournament Course, then confirm cart, practice, any forecaddie charge and tax.

**Access:** The operator explicitly opens the Tournament Course to the public; the Member Course is separate.

**Break 90 approach:** Use generous approach targets and check the carry distances to trouble. A familiar club from a sensible tee is a better start than trying to imitate a professional.

[Official course information](https://www.golfclubofhouston.com/golf/tournament-course)

## Plan Texas golf by region

Dallas–Fort Worth, Austin, San Antonio, Houston, East Texas, Lubbock and the Big Bend area are different travel bases. Start with the city or region you will actually visit. Black Jack’s Crossing and El Paso belong to far-west itineraries, not a casual add-on to an Austin weekend.

For several days in one location, compare the two Fields Ranch courses or the Barton Creek options. Check which public or guest booking window applies to you, and include the required lodging and caddie costs before deciding that one green fee is better value.

Check the forecast, wind, heat, daylight and any course-maintenance notice. Later tee times can be cheaper, but a discount does not guarantee that you can finish 18 holes. Ask about the final checkout total, walking/cart rules and what happens if weather interrupts play.

On the course, use the [math of breaking 90](/articles/break-90-golf-scoring-math-find-lost-strokes/) and choose tees for your normal carry distance. Keep the next shot playable and let the destination be memorable without making every shot heroic.
